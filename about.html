About

Self-employed since 2018. Websites are the third trade I've learned.

I'm Steve. For eight years I've run my own local businesses in and around Wallington, and I still do.

There have been two strands to that. One is a trade business: quoting jobs, turning up when I said I would, doing the work properly and standing behind it afterwards. The other is health and fitness: I'm a personal trainer and a sports massage therapist, working with clients one to one.

On paper those look like different careers. In practice they're the same job in different clothes. In both, you live and die on whether the phone rings, whether people trust you with their money, and whether the work holds up after you've been paid. Nobody hands you customers. You earn every one, and you keep them by being straight with them.

Somewhere along the way I got fed up watching good local businesses get bad websites. I'd see it constantly: a café owner quoted four grand for a template, a plumber paying monthly for a site he didn't own, a salon handed something half-finished by someone who then stopped answering the phone. People who'd never accept that treatment from a tradesman were getting it as standard from web companies, usually wrapped in enough jargon that they couldn't tell whether they were being ripped off. They usually were.

So I learned to build websites properly, and I run Keystone Digital exactly the way I've run every other job since 2018. You get a fixed written quote before anything starts. You get a clear timeline. You get plain English. If I can't explain what you're paying for in one sentence, you shouldn't be paying for it. And you get me afterwards, because not disappearing after launch is half the point.

The thing that makes this work isn't a design qualification. It's that I'm not an agency selling to local businesses. I am a local business, and I have been for eight years. I know what it feels like when the phone doesn't ring. I know what a fair quote looks like and what a dodgy one looks like, because I've written the first kind and been handed the second. Every choice on your website gets made by someone who's stood where you're standing.

I'll be straight about the other thing too: Keystone Digital is a growing business, not a forty-person agency. That's a trade worth understanding. You deal with me directly, start to finish. I answer the phone. Your job is never handed to a junior, and you're never client #400 in somebody's queue. I'm building a reputation locally, which means every single site has to be good enough to be the one I'm judged on, including yours.
